## Key content captured

### What is self-esteem?
- "The opinion we have of ourselves"
- Healthy self-esteem: positive about ourselves and life; better able to deal with ups and downs
- Low self-esteem: more negative and critical view of self and life; less able to take on challenges

### What causes low self-esteem?
- Often begins in childhood
- Teachers, friends, siblings, parents, media send positive and negative messages
- "The message that you are not good enough is the one that stays with you"
- Difficulty living up to others' or own expectations
- Stress and difficult life events (serious illness, bereavement)
- Personality: some people more prone to negative thinking; others set impossibly high standards

### How does low self-esteem affect us?
- Avoidance: hide from social situations, stop trying new things, avoid challenges
- Short-term avoidance feels safe
- Long-term: backfires — reinforces underlying doubts and fears; teaches the unhelpful rule that "the only way to cope is by avoiding things"
- Can harm mental health → depression and anxiety
- May develop unhelpful coping habits (smoking, drinking too much)

### How to have healthy self-esteem (identify and challenge)
- Identify negative beliefs about yourself, then challenge them
- Examples: "too stupid to apply for a new job", "nobody cares about you"
- Note negative thoughts on paper or diary; ask when you first started thinking them
- Write evidence that challenges these beliefs: e.g., "I'm really good at cryptic crosswords", "My sister calls for a chat every week"
- Write other positive things about yourself: "I'm thoughtful", "I'm a great cook", "I'm someone that others trust"
- Include good things other people say about you
- Aim for at least 5 positive things; add regularly; put list somewhere visible
- "We can grow and develop new ways of seeing ourselves at any age"

### Other techniques

**Recognise what you're good at**
- Cooking, singing, puzzles, being a friend
- Doing what you're good at boosts mood

**Build positive relationships**
- Spend less time with people who bring you down (or tell them how you feel)
- Build relationships with people who are positive and appreciate you

**Be kind to yourself**
- Be gentle when you feel self-critical
- "Think what you'd say to a friend in a similar situation. We often give far better advice to others than we do to ourselves."

**Learn to be assertive**
- Respecting others' opinions and needs, and expecting the same from them
- Watch assertive people, copy what they do
- Not about pretending — picking up hints from people you admire and "letting the real you come out"

**Start saying "no"**
- People with low self-esteem feel they have to say yes
- Risk: overburdened, resentful, angry, depressed
- Saying no usually does not upset relationships
- Keep saying no in different ways until they get the message

**Give yourself a challenge**
- People with healthy self-esteem don't let nervousness stop them
- Set a goal (exercise class, social occasion); achieving goals increases self-esteem

### Where to find help
- Talking therapies (counselling or CBT)
- NHS talking therapies self-referral
- Talk to a GP first if preferred
- Private therapist must be registered with a professional body
