## Key content captured

### Section 1 — What is low self-esteem?

**Why self-esteem matters:**
- Self-esteem = how you value yourself as a person
- Affects mind, body, relationships
- Influences daily choices, feeling in new situations, how you appear to others
- "Positive self-esteem is both a cause and a result of healthy living"
- Healthy self-esteem → balanced moods, positive energy, openness to communication, willingness to take on challenges, less blaming others, can accept criticism, learns new skills, secure in honest relationships, deals with stress

**Characteristics of low self-esteem:**
- Social withdrawal
- Anxiety and emotional turmoil
- Lack of social skills and self-confidence
- Depression or bouts of sadness
- Eating disorders
- Inability to accept compliments
- An inability to be fair to yourself
- Accentuating the negative
- Exaggerated concern over what you imagine others think
- Self-neglect
- Treating yourself badly (but not others)
- Worrying whether you have treated others badly
- Reluctance to take on challenges
- Reluctance to put yourself first or anywhere
- Reluctance to trust your own opinion
- Expecting little out of life for yourself

**Self-esteem vs self-confidence:**
- Self-esteem: overall sense of self-worth or personal value; image of self and accomplishments; appraisal of appearance, beliefs, emotions, behaviours
- Self-confidence: self-assurance in trusting abilities, capacities, judgements; varies situation to situation
- Possible to have healthy self-esteem but low confidence in maths (example)

**How low self-esteem affects you:**
- Underlying cause of and links with depression
- Feeling there are things you can't do well (social skills, success, relationships, courage to try new things)
- Hopeless about situation and future
- Automatically think others see them the same way: laughing, rejecting, disrespecting, taking advantage
- Watch for signs, overly sensitive, easily offended
- Watch others to figure out what to do, say, wear
- Behaviours change as situations change → confusion, anxiety, fear of rejection
- Assertiveness lacking → passive until anger builds → can become aggressive (defensive, sarcastic, brusque, rude, violent)
- Passive-aggressive responses: manipulation, planned tardiness, cues, insinuations, gossip
- Overachievers (proving adequacy) or underachievers (unsatisfying jobs/relationships, give up on dreams)
- Self-defeating behaviours repeated

**External factors and self-worth — the "babies" exercise:**
- All babies born equal worth
- Materialistic features "stick" to us: cars, jobs, money, social relationships, marriage, life events, travel, success
- Eventually we lose sense of "core worth" and compare exterior worth to others'
- Need to step back and remember: we're still worth the same as others

**The Wall metaphor:**
- Self-esteem = a wall
- Life events knock it down; we can build it up again
- Not a fixed thing; can be built brick by brick

**Three components of self-esteem:**
- Sense of value (e.g., I am liked)
- Sense of competency (e.g., I can)
- Sense of control (e.g., I will)

**Factors that may lower self-esteem:**
- Being bullied or abused
- Losing job / difficulty finding employment
- Ongoing stress
- Physical illness
- Mental health problems
- Difficult relationship, separation, divorce

**Focus on positives exercise:**
- Write a list of things you like about yourself
- Include ways you feel valued, things you're good at, things you want to achieve
- Ask a friend or loved one to help if difficult

### Section 2 — How to combat negative thinking patterns

Unhelpful thinking patterns occur "just before or during distressing situations." Recognising them is the first step; then challenge.

**Examples of unhelpful thinking patterns:**

- **Negative mental filter**: notice only the negative; dismiss anything that doesn't fit; "gloomy specs"; e.g., noticing only criticism, not praise
- **Negative judgements**: making evaluations/judgements about ourselves rather than describing what we actually see; e.g., judging that others think negatively of us
- **Making assumptions**: assuming we know what others are thinking (usually about us)
- **Compare and despair**: seeing only good in others; comparing self negatively
- **Critical self**: putting self down, self-criticism, blaming self for events not (totally) our fault
- **Black and white thinking**: only good/bad, right/wrong, nothing in between
- **Should and must statements**: increases pressure, unrealistic expectations

### Section 3 — Building self-esteem

**Affirmations:**
- Positive affirmation = repeating a positive statement that includes a belief or intention
- Repeat enough → start to believe it → changes how you think about yourself
- Example affirmations:
  - I deserve to be happy and successful
  - I have the power to change myself
  - I can forgive and understand others
  - I can make my own choices and decisions
  - It is enough to have done my best
  - I deserve to be loved

**Goal setting:**
- Self-esteem reflects relationship with yourself
- Trust, respect, interest, affection → better self-esteem
- Hold yourself accountable in a respectful and constructive way
- Setting goals = practising self-accountability
- "Self-esteem is enhanced when you set a goal that is meaningful and important to you, and you follow through to make it happen"
- Exercise: list 5–10 things you don't like about your life or want to change → opposite of each = goal candidates
- Use SMART format (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound — implied by SMART label)
- Reflect on why goal matters and what you need to do

### Section 4 — Dealing with setbacks and moving forward

- Setbacks are part of life
- Self-esteem setback can be damaging — can spiral downwards
- Recognise warning signs early; act quickly
- Look back through work done and skills learned
- Get support; don't be afraid to seek professional help

**Things to remember (paraphrased points in source):**
- Self-esteem setbacks are expected (everyone experiences them)
- You're moving forwards (general direction is forward; acknowledge progress)
- You haven't failed (setback ≠ failure)
- Don't be hard on yourself (nobody's perfect; self-care is crucial)
- You will get through this (setbacks are temporary)
- Learn from the experience (growth opportunity; ask what the setback is telling you)
- A self-esteem setback is not the end of the world

### Adjustments for TR product

- "Affirmations" framing must be CBT-grounded (evidence-based reframe, not magical positive thinking). Use the user's own evidence list rather than scripted affirmations alone.
- The Oxford booklet includes social-comparison and "compare and despair" content that maps well to Turkish social media context — but we must avoid stigmatising any specific platform.
- Eating disorders listed as a characteristic of low self-esteem — we must flag this as a separate referral path (not a self-help target in this module).
- Domestic violence and abuse mentioned in source as possible behavioural outcomes — when describing aggression/passive-aggression we must not pathologise users; this content is for awareness, not labelling.
- The references in this booklet include UK-specific Mind link and goodtherapy.org — NOT transferable.

**Critical safety addition:** The Oxford self-esteem workbook does not contain an explicit suicide screening question. Our self-esteem module safety card must include:
- Explicit suicide screening (low self-esteem is a depression risk factor and per Oxford explicitly "underlying cause of and links with depression")
- 112 + family doctor / psikiyatri / klinik psikolog routing
- Eating-disorder symptoms → separate referral (not self-help)
- Domestic/intimate partner violence → separate referral
