## Key content captured

Definition:
- GAD = common mental health condition where you often feel very anxious about lots of different things
- Page is about adults aged 18+; children's anxiety is covered separately

Symptoms of GAD:
- Main symptom: feelings of stress or worry that affect daily life and are difficult to control
- Other symptoms:
  - difficulty sleeping
  - feeling restless, irritable or finding it difficult to concentrate
  - getting tired easily
  - feeling tense
  - stomach problems
  - more noticeable or unusual heartbeat (palpitations)
  - feeling lightheaded or dizzy
  - low mood or depression
- Common to have more than one anxiety disorder at the same time (panic disorder, phobias, etc.)

"If you're not sure it's GAD":
- Most people feel anxious sometimes. It does not always mean you have GAD.
- More likely to be GAD if you feel anxious most of the time and it affects everyday life.

When to see a GP:
- If you think you might have GAD
- If you've been diagnosed with GAD and treatments are not helping
- In the UK, can also self-refer to NHS talking therapies (NOT applicable in TR — do not transfer)

How GAD is diagnosed (by GP):
- Wide range of worries
- Anxiety affects daily life
- Difficulty controlling feelings
- Anxious most of the time for at least 6 months

Treatments mentioned:
- Talking therapies — usually CBT, individual or group
- Antidepressants (usually SSRI)
- Referral to community mental health team

GP usually advises talking therapies before medicine or specialist referral.

Self-help "Do":
- talk about feelings to friend/family/health professional/counsellor (Samaritans 116 123 mentioned — UK-only, DO NOT transfer)
- recommended self-help (books, online tools, courses)
- calming breathing exercises, yoga, pilates
- get enough sleep
- exercise regularly (running, walking, swimming, yoga)
- healthy diet with regular meals

Self-help "Don't":
- do not focus on things you cannot change
- do not avoid situations that make you anxious — try slowly building up time in worrying situations to gradually reduce anxiety
- do not drink lots of caffeine (coffee, tea, cola, energy drinks)
- do not use alcohol, cigarettes, gambling or drugs to relieve anxiety

Causes / risk factors:
- another anxiety disorder (panic, social phobia)
- close relatives with anxiety / depression / other MH conditions
- history of stressful or traumatic experiences (domestic violence, child abuse, bullying)
- painful long-term health condition (e.g. arthritis)
- history of drug or alcohol misuse
- more common in women than men
