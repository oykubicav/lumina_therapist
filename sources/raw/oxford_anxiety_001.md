## Key content captured

### Part 1. Understanding Anxiety

Anxiety is a normal reaction — designed to keep us safe by:
- keeping us alert to spot/avoid danger
- preparing our bodies for action
- helping us learn to stay out of future danger

Fight or flight response — 3 core parts:
1. **How we think**: identifying threat/danger; what we think will happen, how things will go wrong
2. **How we feel**: body's prep response — racing heart, hot, short of breath, going red, tense, on edge
3. **What we do**: keeping safe — often by avoiding situations we worry about

Anxious thinking patterns:
- We will be unable to cope
- We will get things wrong
- Bad things will happen
- People will laugh at us / be unkind
- We look for signs of danger

Anxiety symptoms (body):
- feeling tense, fast breathing, headaches, feeling dizzy, going red/blushing, alert, shaking, feeling hot, butterflies, racing heart, dry mouth, sweating, wanting to go to toilet

Other symptoms:
- difficulty concentrating, not wanting to go out, problems sleeping, not eating, feeling unwell and sick, being irritable, temper outbursts, thinking about fears all the time

The Anxiety Trap (the maintaining cycle):
- Worrying thoughts (can't cope, things will go wrong) → Anxious feelings (body changes) → Avoidance → reinforces worrying thoughts

Effects of anxiety on life:
- Limits what you do and where you go
- Strain on relationships
- Limits social and relaxation time

Types of anxiety mentioned:
- Generalised anxiety — worry about anything and everything, all the time
- Social anxiety
- Panic attacks
- Phobias

Causes:
- Genetics (predisposition)
- Life events (stress, bullying, illness, bereavement, moving)
- Family factors (anxious parents, learned coping)
- Character (shyness, worry tendency)

### Part 2. Learning to Beat Anxiety (5 CBT steps)

**The goal:** to learn to cope with anxiety, NOT to get rid of it. No one is anxiety-free. Anxiety can be helpful.

**5 steps:**
1. Understanding anxiety (diary, body cues)
2. Learning to stay calm (relaxation, breathing, building a toolbox)
3. Identifying and challenging anxious thoughts ("Catch it, Check it, Challenge it, Change it")
4. Facing your fears (exposure ladder / small steps)
5. Learn to solve problems

#### Step 1: Understanding Your Anxiety
- Diary: how often, where/when, what triggers, what thoughts, what you do
- Look for patterns: particular days/times, common situations, common thoughts, strongest body signals
- Body cues / signals — recognising before overwhelm

#### Step 2: Staying Calm
- Many ways to relax; no one universal method
- Build a toolbox of techniques
- Practice regularly (e.g. before bed)

#### Step 3: Identifying & Challenging Anxious Thoughts
- Negative thought patterns: "won't be successful", "won't cope", "bad things will happen", "people will judge me"
- Rumination = thoughts tumbling in head, hours lost
- Counter: search actively for evidence of coping and success
- Positivity journal — log 2-3 positive things daily (coped, faced anxiety, tried new, were successful)
- **Catch it, Check it, Challenge it, Change it (4 Cs):**
  1. Catch the unhelpful thought
  2. Check it — fears justified? Exaggerating? What evidence is there?
  3. Challenge it — evidence for/against, any positives overlooked
  4. Change it — more balanced and helpful way of thinking
- "What would my best friend / support person say?" technique
- Questions to ask:
  - Is this thought helpful?
  - Has this happened before? How many times?
  - How likely is it to happen?
  - What else could happen?
  - Am I only focusing on the negative?
  - What would I say to someone else thinking this?

#### Step 4: Face Your Fears (exposure ladder / small steps)
- Avoiding makes it worse
- Exposure ladder: break feared situation into small, evenly-spaced steps
- Easiest at bottom, hardest at top
- Plan:
  1. Identify goal and plan steps
  2. Catch unhelpful thoughts about each step; check and challenge
  3. Develop positive coping thoughts
  4. Use relaxation if anxiety builds
  5. Visualise success
  6. Find day/time; use support person
  7. Recognise success, give credit, reward
- Learnings from facing fears:
  - You can cope with anxiety
  - Anxiety is not as bad as you imagine
  - Anxious feelings fade over time

#### Step 5: Problem Solving (6 steps)
1. What is the problem?
2. What are the possible solutions?
3. What are the consequences of each solution?
4. On balance, what is the best solution?
5. Try it
6. Did it work?

### Toolbox summary
- Know your body cues
- Keep a diary
- Catch, check, challenge, change anxious thinking
- Take small steps
- Face your fears
- Problem solve
- Find a support person to talk with

## Use in our product

This is the most CBT-systematic of the 3 GAD sources. The 5-step structure (understand / stay calm / challenge thinking / face fears / problem solve), the 4 Cs cognitive restructuring, and the exposure ladder are standard adult CBT techniques and map directly onto chatbot interactions. The booklet's worry/avoidance trap is the canonical maintaining cycle. We use the model but rewrite examples for adults (work, finance, relationships, health, future) instead of school/homework/friends.

**Crucially missing for GAD-specific CBT:** This booklet does NOT cover the worry-specific CBT distinctions central to adult GAD treatment:
- Type 1 vs Type 2 worry (worry about real things vs worry about worrying)
- Practical worry (problem-solvable) vs hypothetical worry ("what if" with no solution)
- Worry postponement / worry time
- Intolerance of uncertainty as the core driver of GAD
These are standard in adult CBT for GAD (Borkovec, Wells, Dugas models) and the NHS/CNTW structural maps imply them ("what keeps anxiety going"), but the actual content is not extracted here. We synthesize these from established CBT-for-GAD framing rather than fabricating from sources.
