## Key content captured

Definition framing:
- Panic disorder = regular sudden attacks of panic/fear
- Distinction: everyone has anxiety/panic sometimes — panic *disorder* is recurrent, unexpected, often "for no apparent reason"
- "Fear of fear" cycle — avoidance fuels more attacks

Panic attack symptoms (NHS list):
- racing heartbeat, feeling faint, sweating, nausea, chest pain, shortness of breath, trembling, hot flushes, chills, shaky limbs, choking sensation, dizziness, numbness/pins and needles, dry mouth, fear of dying, churning stomach, tingling fingers, depersonalisation ("feeling like you're not connected to your body")

Course:
- Most attacks 5-20 minutes; some up to an hour
- Frequency varies (1-2/month to several/week)
- "Attacks are frightening but not dangerous; not life-threatening, won't cause physical harm"
- Caveat: many symptoms overlap with other conditions, so first-time symptoms need medical evaluation

When to see GP — recurrent symptoms; GP may do physical exam to rule out other conditions.
Diagnostic threshold (informational): regular unexpected attacks + at least a month of continuous worry/concern about further attacks.

Treatments:
- Talking therapies (CBT) — self-referral possible
- Medication: SSRIs (or tricyclics like imipramine/clomipramine); pregabalin or clonazepam for anxiety
- Referral to psychiatrist/clinical psychologist if no response

What to do during a panic attack (NHS list):
- stay where you are if possible
- breathe slowly and deeply
- remind yourself the attack will pass
- focus on positive, peaceful, relaxing images
- remember it's not life-threatening

Prevention:
- self-help CBT book (ask GP)
- yoga, pilates, breathing exercises
- regular exercise
- avoid sugar, caffeine, alcohol; stop smoking
- support groups (Anxiety UK, Mind, No Panic, TOP UK)

Complications: untreated panic disorder can escalate; risk of agoraphobia, phobias, alcohol/drug problems. DVLA notification may be required.

Causes (multifactorial): traumatic/stressful life experience, family history, neurotransmitter imbalance.
