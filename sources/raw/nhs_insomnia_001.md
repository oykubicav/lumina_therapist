## Key content captured

### What is insomnia?
- "Insomnia means you regularly have problems sleeping."
- Usually gets better by changing sleeping habits.
- Short-term insomnia = <3 months; long-term insomnia = ≥3 months.

### Symptoms of insomnia (regular pattern of):
- Hard to go to sleep
- Waking several times during the night
- Lying awake at night
- Waking early, cannot go back to sleep
- Still feeling tired after waking
- Hard to nap during the day even though tired
- Feeling tired and irritable during the day
- Difficulty concentrating during the day

### How much sleep needed
- Adults: 7 to 9 hours
- Children: 9 to 13 hours
- Toddlers and babies: 12 to 17 hours
- "Probably not getting enough sleep if constantly tired during the day."

### What causes insomnia
Most common:
- Stress, anxiety or depression
- Noise
- Room too hot or cold
- Uncomfortable beds
- Alcohol, caffeine or nicotine
- Illegal drugs like cocaine or ecstasy
- Jet lag
- Shift work

Conditions that can cause insomnia:
- Mental health conditions (schizophrenia, bipolar disorder)
- Alzheimer's disease or Parkinson's disease
- Restless legs syndrome
- Overactive thyroid
- Menopause

Things that keep you from getting good night's sleep:
- Long-term pain
- Sleepwalking
- Snoring or interrupted breathing while sleeping (sleep apnoea)
- Suddenly falling asleep anywhere (narcolepsy)
- Nightmares or night terrors (particularly in children)

Many medicines can cause insomnia.

### Self-treatment — Do
- Only go to bed when sleepy
- Wake up and get out of bed at the same time every day
- Relax at least 1 hour before bed (bath, book)
- Bedroom dark and quiet (curtains, blinds, eye mask, ear plugs)
- Exercise regularly during the day
- Comfortable mattress, pillows, covers

### Self-treatment — Don't
- Do not smoke or drink alcohol, tea or coffee at least 6 hours before bed
- Do not eat big meal late at night
- Do not exercise at least 4 hours before bed
- Do not watch TV or use devices right before bed (blue light)
- Do not nap during the day
- Do not drive when sleepy
- Do not sleep in after a bad night — stick to regular hours

### Pharmacy help
- OTC tablets/liquids (sometimes called sleeping aids): valerian, lavender, antihistamines
- Cannot cure insomnia; may help 1-2 weeks; should not be taken longer
- Side effects (drowsiness); check with doctor first

### When to see GP (non-urgent)
- Changing sleeping habits hasn't helped
- Trouble sleeping for months
- Insomnia affecting daily life

### Treatment from GP
- GP tries to find cause
- **CBT** may be offered (face-to-face or online self-help programme)
- CBT can help change thoughts and behaviours that keep you from sleeping
- May refer to sleep clinic if another sleep disorder (e.g., sleep apnoea) suspected
- **GPs now rarely prescribe sleeping pills** — serious side effects, dependency
- Sleeping pills only for a few days or weeks at most, if insomnia very bad and other treatments haven't worked
