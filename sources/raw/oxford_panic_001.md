## Key content captured

Definition framing:
- Panic attacks happen when the body thinks it senses a threat
- Activation of fight-or-flight response → racing heart, breathlessness, dizziness, nausea, sweating, hot/cold
- Feels intense — people sometimes think they are dying — but there is no underlying physical problem causing them (once a panic-attack diagnosis is established)
- Anticipatory fear of having another panic attack is itself part of the problem

Course:
- Worst in the first 10 minutes
- Typically lasts 30-45 minutes total
- Will pass without treatment

In-attack coping strategies:
- Slow breathing 5 seconds in / 5 seconds out
- Abdominal (tummy) breathing — hand on tummy, push out on inhale, draw in on exhale
- Sensory grounding: chew flavoured gum/sweets, wrap in a coat/blanket and focus on the weight, walk barefoot to focus on ground sensation

After a panic attack:
- Tell a trusted person if possible
- Take care of yourself, do something relaxing (often feel tired)
- Apps: Beat Panic, Stress & Anxiety Companion
- Support lines: Mind 0300 123 3393, Anxiety UK 03444 775 774 (UK numbers — do not transfer to TR product)

When to see GP: frequent panic attacks affecting day-to-day life. Bring a symptom diary.
GP may offer antidepressants or CBT (computer-based or in person); self-referral for CBT possible.

Crisis: Samaritans 116 123 mentioned (UK).
