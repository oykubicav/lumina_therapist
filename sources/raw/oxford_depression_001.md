## Key content captured (substantial — this is the rich CBT-for-depression source)

### Section 1 — Understand and recognise symptoms

Screening questions:
- Is your mood low for large periods of the day, generally "down in the dumps"?
- Have your energy levels and/or motivation for things you previously enjoyed decreased?
- Do you often feel weepy or irritable?
- Do things feel like they're getting on top of you?
- Has your confidence reduced?
- Have you been experiencing suicidal thoughts? ← **important triage item**
- Has your sleep pattern changed?
- Has your appetite or concentration changed?

Framing of "normal low" vs depression:
- Everyone has times when they feel low or unmotivated
- Depression is a more extreme form, lasting an extended period
- Depression typically affects motivation, appetite, sleep, concentration, memory
- Often coexists with anxiety, irritability, weepiness, isolation
- Common and treatable

Contributory factors:
- Loss (bereavement, life changes, retirement, empty nest)
- Traumatic life events (job loss, divorce, assault)
- Biological / familial (family history → increased likelihood)
- Behaviour pattern shifts (staying indoors, reduced social contact, daily activities becoming difficult)

### Section 2 — Coping strategies (behavioural activation core)

Core CBT model: negative life events → low mood → reduced activity / unhealthy coping (sleep late, alcohol/drugs, social withdrawal) → less pleasurable activities → less positive reinforcement → depression maintained.

Negative behaviour patterns:
- Cognitive avoidance: not thinking about relationship problems, not making decisions, not taking opportunities, not being serious about work/study, ruminating about past or unsolvable problems
- Avoidance by distraction: gaming, gambling, comfort eating, excessive exercise
- Social withdrawal: not answering phone, avoiding friends
- Non-social avoidance: not taking on challenging tasks, sitting around, excessive time in bed

Positive replacement behaviours:
- Socialising (even when you don't feel like it)
- Hobbies and interests
- Exercise (doesn't have to be intense — walking)
- Regular bedtimes
- Time for yourself / relaxation

Rating scale used:
- Ease (0 very hard — 10 very easy)
- Reward (0 not rewarding — 10 very rewarding)

Implementation: activity scheduling — choose specific days/times, plan obstacles, start with easy/rewarding activities, gradually add more difficult ones, record actual time you get up and go to bed.

### Distraction techniques (for acute low mood)
- Watch film/TV
- Read a book
- Music
- Exercise
- Draw
- **Caveat in source**: "distractions are not a cure for depression" — they give respite, but facing causes and accepting emotions matter for healing. Find balance.

### Organisation (productive distraction)
- Clean/order spaces
- Organise calendar
- Handle finances
- Structure exercise

### Comedy and humour
- Funny film/TV
- Favourite comedian
- Funny book/magazine

### Challenge negative thinking
- "Think outside yourself": would you say what you're thinking about yourself to someone else? If not, less harsh statement.
- Allow yourself to be less than perfect (perfectionism)
- Socialise with positive people, notice how they handle setbacks
- Keep a negative thought log; review when in better mood; consider if negativity was warranted, alternative views

### Additional coping strategies
- Turn to trusted friends/family for emotional connection (listeners, not fixers)
- Keep up social activities even when you don't feel like it
- Support groups
- Exercise: "as effective as antidepressant medication" for energy/fatigue (per major studies cited)
- Relaxation: yoga, deep breathing, progressive muscle relaxation, meditation
- Healthy lifestyle, stress management, setting limits, healthy habits, scheduling fun
- Aim for 8 hours sleep
- 15 minutes sunlight daily; light therapy box if dark winter
- Care for a pet
- Pick up former hobbies; express creatively; push to do things

### Section 3 — Road to recovery

Wellness toolbox examples:
- List what you like about yourself
- Read book / magazine with pictures
- Funny film/TV
- Long hot bath, manicure, self-massage
- Tackle small achievable tasks
- Pet
- Talk to friends face-to-face
- Music
- Crossword/word search/colouring/knitting/jigsaw

Diet:
- Don't skip meals; eat every 3-4 hours
- Minimise sugar / refined carbs
- Focus on complex carbs (potatoes, whole wheat, oatmeal, whole grain bread) for serotonin
- Vitamins/minerals — folic acid, B-12; B-complex supplement; citrus, leafy greens, beans, chicken, eggs
- Hydration; avoid alcohol (temporary lift, worse long-term); avoid excessive caffeine (disturbs sleep, mood swings)

### Section 4 — Dealing with setbacks (relapse prevention)

Common triggers for relapse:
- Not following treatment plan (especially stopping medication without GP discussion — **clinical caution: do not stop medication on your own; talk to doctor**)
- Not practising therapeutic techniques
- Dwelling on flaws/failures, viewing life through negative lens
- Knowing personal vulnerabilities (anniversaries, divorce, death/loss dates on calendar)
- Checking in with physical state — sleep, appetite, concentration, energy

Warning signs / checklist of past symptoms:
- Tearfulness, irritability, anxiety — early warning patterns

Crisis planning:
- Prevention/crisis plan helps manage recovery
- Monitor past symptoms, reflect, be stress aware
- Recognise own triggers and symptoms
- Think ahead, talk with friends, family, GP for early support
- List contacts for early support

### Depressive cognitive triad (final passage):
- Negative way of looking at self, world, and futures
- Negative thinking continues depression — clouds interpretation, maintains negative outlook
- Reduced activity → less pleasure → little to look forward to → keeps low mood
- Inactivity → lethargy → harder to escape — vicious cycle
- Excessive bed-time and TV → feeling time-wasted, achieved little → feels worse, more rumination time
- Wellness toolbox + activity scheduling = positive moving forward
