## Sections referenced

Section 1 — About health anxiety
- Definition: intense anxiety about one's health, fear or belief of serious illness, distress + functional interference
- Common physical symptoms reported: chest pain, aches, sweating, trembling, headaches, numbness, tingling, noticed bodily change (lump/bump)
- Core mechanism: misinterpretation of body sensations as severe illness
- Physiological arousal feeds the cycle
- Behaviours: body checking, reassurance-seeking (doctors, family/friends), spending time researching illness, avoiding illness content, avoiding physical activity
- Case example: "Mary, 36, physiotherapist, worried about lung cancer" — used only to learn the pattern, not to be reused in our product
- Symptoms of anxiety listed: heart rate up, palpitations, chest pain, breathlessness, sweating, dry mouth, dizziness, tingling, aches, tiredness, shaky legs, trembling, digestive disturbance, headaches

Section 2 — What can be done to decrease health anxiety
- List the strategies tried and their short vs long term effectiveness
- "Stop trying to prove that nothing is wrong with you" — reassurance never lasts
- Dealing with worries: explore alternative explanations (stress, normal body changes), evidence-for / evidence-against table, thought chart with rating 0–100
- 5-minute focused-attention experiment: focusing on a symptom intensifies it; redirecting attention reduces it — used to demonstrate that body-focus aggravates symptoms
- Stop asking for reassurance — keep a diary of frequency, ask family/friends to NOT give reassurance, use distraction
- Stop checking and monitoring — agree what is reasonable (e.g., monthly self-checks, not daily), use distraction
- Stop finding out about illness (Dr Google, medical books)
- Stop avoiding illness-related content — graded exposure starting with the easiest item
- What else are you avoiding — physical activity loss → deconditioning → more symptoms → more worry → more avoidance

Distraction toolkit examples: physical activity, attention shifts (describe a room in detail), mental arithmetic (count back from 500 by 7s), talking to someone about something else.

Goal frame: the aim is **not** to eliminate symptoms — bodies always have sensations — but to reduce worry about those sensations.
