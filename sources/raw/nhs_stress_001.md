## Framing (direct)

"Most people feel stressed sometimes and some people find stress helpful or even motivating. But if stress is affecting your life, there are things you can try that may help."

Key nuance: stress is not automatically pathological — problem is chronic exposure + insufficient coping.

## Symptom taxonomy captured

**Physical:**
- headaches or dizziness
- muscle tension or pain
- stomach problems
- chest pain or a faster heartbeat
- sexual problems

**Mental:**
- difficulty concentrating
- struggling to make decisions
- feeling overwhelmed
- constantly worrying
- being forgetful

**Behavioural changes:**
- being irritable and snappy
- sleeping too much or too little
- eating too much or too little
- avoiding certain places or people
- drinking or smoking more

## Cause categorization

- work — pressure, unemployment, retirement
- family — relationship difficulties, divorce, caring role
- financial problems — bills, borrowing
- health — illness, injury, bereavement
- major life events — buying a house, having a baby, planning a wedding

## Do / Don't lists

Do:
- Talk to a friend, family, or health professional
- 10 stress busters (movement + time for yourself)
- Time management
- Breathing exercises
- Plan ahead for stressful days
- Peer support
- Mental wellbeing audio guides

Don't:
- Try to do everything at once — set small targets
- Focus on things you cannot change
- Tell yourself you're alone
- Use alcohol / cigarettes / gambling / drugs to relieve stress

## Physiology framing

"When you're feeling anxious or scared, your body releases stress hormones such as adrenaline and cortisol. This can be helpful for some people... But it might also cause physical symptoms."
