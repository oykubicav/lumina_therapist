## Section 1 — Conceptual frame: what low self-esteem is and is not

**Definition (NHS + Oxford):**
Self-esteem is the overall opinion or valuation we hold of ourselves — how much positive regard, worth, and self-love we carry. Healthy self-esteem means feeling generally positive about ourselves and capable of dealing with life's ups and downs. Low self-esteem means seeing ourselves and our lives in a more negative, critical light, feeling less able to take on challenges.

**Self-esteem vs self-confidence (Oxford):**
Self-esteem = global self-worth (cuts across contexts).
Self-confidence = trust in your abilities in a specific situation (varies situation-to-situation).
A person can have healthy self-esteem but low confidence about, say, maths. The two are related but separable. In Turkish: "öz-değer" (self-worth/self-esteem) vs "öz-güven" (self-confidence) — useful distinction in user-facing language.

**Three components of self-esteem (Oxford):**
- Sense of value: "I am liked / I matter" (öz-değer)
- Sense of competency: "I can" (yeterlilik)
- Sense of control: "I will" (eyleme geçebilme)

**Wall metaphor (Oxford):**
Self-esteem is not a fixed personality trait. It is more like a wall — life events can knock it down, but it can be built up again brick by brick. This is a hopeful, change-oriented frame.

**Scope (NHS Inform Scotland):**
CBT-based self-help is designed for mild-to-moderate difficulties. Severe distress, dysfunction, comorbid eating disorder, ongoing trauma, or suicidal ideation all need professional evaluation, not self-help alone.

## Section 2 — Origins and maintaining cycle

**Origins (NHS + Oxford):**
- Often begins in childhood: messages from teachers, friends, siblings, parents, media
- "The message that you are not good enough is the one that stays with you" (NHS)
- Difficulty meeting others' or own expectations
- Stress and difficult life events: serious illness, bereavement
- Personality: some more prone to negative thinking; others set impossibly high standards
- Adult triggers (Oxford): bullying or abuse, job loss / unemployment, ongoing stress, physical illness, mental health problems, difficult relationship / separation / divorce

**Core maintaining cycle (NHS — explicit; Oxford — compatible):**

1. Underlying belief: "I'm not good enough / I'm flawed / I'm unlovable / I don't deserve good things"
2. Triggering situation: social context, challenge, evaluation
3. Negative automatic thoughts: "They'll laugh at me", "I'll mess this up", "I'm boring"
4. Emotional and bodily response: anxiety, shame, sadness, tension
5. Behavioural response: avoidance (hide from social situations, stop trying new things, avoid challenges) OR safety behaviours (people-pleasing, over-apologising, perfectionism, saying yes when you want to say no)
6. Short-term consequence: relief, feels safe
7. Long-term consequence: belief is NEVER disconfirmed; avoidance teaches the unhelpful rule "the only way to cope is by avoiding things"; resentment builds from over-saying-yes; cycle is maintained

**Critical insight (NHS direct):** Long-term avoidance "backfires because it reinforces your underlying doubts and fears." Approach (with care) is the cure for avoidance.

**Linked outcomes (NHS + Oxford):**
- Depression and anxiety (low self-esteem is an upstream factor for both)
- Unhelpful coping: smoking, drinking, possible disordered eating
- Passive-aggressive patterns, sometimes flips to aggression
- Overachievement (proving adequacy) or underachievement (giving up dreams)

## Section 3 — Self-check / recognition

**Characteristics of low self-esteem (Oxford, selected & softened for user-facing TR):**
- Hiding from social situations / social withdrawal
- Difficulty accepting compliments
- Talking to yourself more harshly than you would talk to a friend
- Exaggerated concern about what others are thinking
- Self-neglect (skipping meals, sleep, healthcare, basic care)
- Avoiding challenges or new things
- Reluctance to put yourself first
- Reluctance to trust your own opinion
- Expecting little out of life for yourself
- Saying yes when you want to say no
- Sustained anxiety / sadness / emotional turmoil tied to how you view yourself

**Self-check NOT used as a diagnostic tool** — it surfaces patterns for the user, and prompts referral if the picture is severe.

**Triage flags (Oxford lists these as possible characteristics; we treat them as referral signals):**
- Eating problems (skipping meals, binge/purge, severe restriction) → separate eating disorder pathway, not this module
- Persistent very low mood, hopelessness → depression module + clinical evaluation
- Self-harm urge or suicidal thoughts → safety card immediately (see Section 5)
- History of abuse / current abusive relationship → not a self-help target; safe support routing required

## Section 4 — Working tools (the CBT toolkit)

### 4.1 Identify-and-challenge negative thoughts (NHS + Oxford)
- Notice negative thoughts about self; write them down in a diary
- Ask when you first started thinking them
- Write evidence that challenges these beliefs (concrete, factual)
- "What would you say to a friend in the same situation?" — leverage the gap between self-talk and friend-talk

### 4.2 Unhelpful thinking patterns to label (Oxford, 7 patterns)
- Negative mental filter (gloomy specs — only see negatives, dismiss positives)
- Negative judgements (judging instead of describing)
- Making assumptions (mind-reading)
- Compare and despair (compare your inside to others' outside; social media context)
- Critical self (self-criticism, self-blame for things not your fault)
- Black and white thinking (all-or-nothing)
- Should / must statements (unrealistic pressure)

Naming the pattern reduces the grip. In TR user-facing language we use plain phrasings: "ya hep ya hiç", "kendine acımasız davranma", "olmadık şeyleri kendine yüklemek", etc.

### 4.3 Positives list / strengths log (NHS)
- Aim for at least 5 positive things about yourself
- Add to it regularly
- Include "good things other people say about you"
- Put it somewhere visible
- This is a behavioural memory aid against the negative mental filter
- Frame as evidence collection, NOT as forced positivity / toxic positivity

### 4.4 Inner critic vs inner ally (NHS + Oxford)
- "What would you say to a friend in the same situation?"
- Be kind to yourself (gentle when self-critical)
- Self-kindness is not self-indulgence; it is a CBT-grounded reframe

### 4.5 Small wins / behavioural challenges (NHS)
- People with healthy self-esteem don't let nervousness stop them
- Set a small specific goal (e.g., join an exercise class, attend a social occasion, send a message you've been postponing)
- Achieving small goals provides disconfirming evidence against "I can't / I'm not good enough"
- Pair with Oxford SMART goal setting: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound

### 4.6 Approach instead of avoid (NHS)
- Avoidance is the central maintaining behaviour
- Each small approach gives evidence against the negative belief
- Build a hierarchy from least feared to most feared; start small

### 4.7 Assertiveness and saying "no" (NHS)
- Assertiveness = respecting others' opinions and needs AND expecting the same from them
- Saying no usually does not damage relationships
- Watch and learn from people you admire who do this well
- For users who routinely say yes against their wishes — this is core work

### 4.8 Affirmations (Oxford — used cautiously)
- Repeating a positive statement that includes a belief or intention
- Useful as cognitive reframe IF grounded in evidence the user can produce
- DO NOT present as magical positive thinking; the evidence list (4.3) is the foundation
- Examples (we adapt for TR): "Mutlu olmayı hak ediyorum", "Değişme gücüm var", "Elimden geleni yapmış olmam yeterli"

### 4.9 SMART goal setting (Oxford)
- Tie goals to what matters to you (meaning increases follow-through)
- List 5–10 things you'd like to change → opposite of each is a candidate goal
- Apply SMART filter
- Reflect on why and what you need to do

## Section 5 — Safety, referral, and Turkey-specific routing

**Suicide screening (mandatory in safety card):**
The Oxford workbook does not embed an explicit suicide screening question. We add one because:
- Oxford itself states low self-esteem is "an underlying cause of and links with depression"
- NHS frames low self-esteem as harmful to mental health and a route into depression and anxiety
- Our depression module screens explicitly; the self-esteem module must as well

The screening item (TR): "Ölmeyi, kendime zarar vermeyi, ya da 'olmasaydım daha iyi olurdu' diye düşünmüş olabilirim". If yes → immediate 112 routing or family doctor / psikiyatri referral.

**Specialist evaluation triggers:**
- Eating-disorder symptoms (skipping meals, binge, purge, severe restriction) → separate evaluation; this module is NOT a substitute
- Current or recent abusive relationship → professional support, not self-help alone
- Severe depression already diagnosed → coordinate with psychiatrist before independent self-help
- Suicidal ideation, plan, or means → 112 / nearest emergency department; do not stay alone

**Turkey routing (the ONLY allowed routing in user-facing cards):**
- 112 — emergency (acil)
- Aile hekimi (family doctor)
- Psikiyatri uzmanı (psychiatrist)
- Klinik psikolog (clinical psychologist)
- "Güvendiğin bir yakın" (trusted close person) — for emotional support, NOT a substitute for professional care

**Forbidden in TR product:**
- NHS 24 / 111 (UK only)
- NHS talking therapies self-referral (UK only)
- Samaritans, Mind, Anxiety UK (UK only)
- 182 (Turkey MHRS — appointment booking line, NOT a crisis line — must not be presented as crisis support)
- Any invented hotline number
- Magical positive thinking framing of affirmations

**Tone guards (apply across all 10 cards):**
- No "merak etme" / "kafana takma" (false reassurance, invalidating)
- No "kafanda kuruyorsun" (invalidating)
- No diagnosis labelling ("sen şöyle birisin")
- Always treat the user as the expert of their own experience
- Always pair difficult content with a small concrete step
- The 10 cards must not contradict each other — the avoidance-is-maintainer message in psychoed must match the approach-small-step message in small wins
